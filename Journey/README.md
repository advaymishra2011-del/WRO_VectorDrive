Our journey throughout WRO Prep has been anything but a straight line. Full of setbacks, late nights, and small victories that made the whole build worth it.

It started with the design phase, where we spent countless late nights on Fusion , iterating over chassis models, tweaking mounts, and redesigning parts that looked perfect on screen but didn't quite fit once printed. Every small change meant re-checking tolerances, re-exporting, and re-printing, a slow, sometimes frustrating process, but one that taught us a lot about designing for the real world instead of just the software.

One morning, we walked into school ready to check on our 3D print in progress, only to find it had mysteriously been swapped out for a pair of tiny Jordans sitting on the print bed. Funny at first, but it turned into a useful reminder to keep a closer eye on shared print space and back up our print jobs.

Hardware brought its own challenges. We spent a long stretch debugging I2C communication issues with the BNO sensor with problems like inconsistent readings and addresses not showing up as expected It took a lot of patience, re-wiring, and re-testing to finally get stable, reliable data out of it.

Then, quite literally hours before out flight, we discovered our PCB wasn't working as expected. With time running out, we improvised, using jumper wires directly on the PCB to bypass faulty connections and keep testing possible. 

Through late nights, failed prints, stubborn sensors, and last-minute hardware fixes, we kept pushing forward and ultimately did our best. Whether or not our bot outperforms everyone, it represents a month of our dedicated focus and application and an unforgettable experience.
